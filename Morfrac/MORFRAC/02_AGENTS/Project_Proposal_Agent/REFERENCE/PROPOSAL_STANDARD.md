# MORFRAC Proposal Standard

## Purpose

This file defines the content, quality, audience, claims, commercial-review and revision standards for MORFRAC proposals.

Workflow execution, Paperclip coordination, persistence, approval gates and external release are defined in `AGENTS.md`.

---

# 1. Proposal Principles

A MORFRAC proposal must be:

- traceable to current project evidence;
- clear about scope and boundaries;
- commercially explicit;
- technically supportable;
- understandable without internal notes;
- clear about assumptions and dependencies;
- revision controlled;
- separated from confidential internal commercial analysis.

Do not strengthen evidence or imply authority that has not been granted.

A proposal draft is not:

- an engineering release;
- a contract;
- an accepted order;
- a purchase commitment;
- a production release;
- evidence that the client has received or accepted anything.

---

# 2. Client Draft and Internal Review Pack

Keep two distinct outputs.

## Client Draft

The client draft contains only information appropriate for the intended client audience.

It must not contain unrelated internal information such as:

- internal labour-cost rates;
- internal margins;
- discount authority;
- supplier confidential terms;
- internal risk strategy;
- legal negotiation strategy;
- private review notes;
- unrelated project information.

## Internal Review Pack

The internal review pack contains the evidence needed to review the client draft.

It should identify, where relevant:

- proposal ID and version;
- project;
- originating issue;
- client-draft path;
- source revisions;
- technical review status;
- schedule review status;
- price/commercial review status;
- legal review status;
- assumptions;
- unresolved decisions;
- deviations;
- confidentiality;
- release readiness.

The client draft and internal review pack should use the same proposal ID and version.

---

# 3. Standard Proposal Structure

A substantive client proposal should normally contain:

## Document Control

- proposal title;
- proposal ID;
- version;
- date;
- project;
- status;
- confidentiality where applicable.

Until externally released by an authorised human process, status remains:

`DRAFT - NOT RELEASED`

or equivalent controlled draft wording.

## Executive Summary

State:

- the client need;
- MORFRAC's proposed response;
- principal deliverables;
- principal commercial basis;
- important limitations.

Keep this consistent with the detailed scope.

## Client Objective

Describe the need or objective supported by the available project evidence.

Do not invent client requirements.

## Proposed Solution

Describe the proposed MORFRAC solution at the level supported by the current technical evidence.

Separate:

- confirmed scope;
- proposed scope;
- assumptions;
- optional work;
- unresolved technical items.

## Scope and Deliverables

Identify:

- included work;
- deliverables;
- quantities where relevant;
- revisions/configurations;
- boundaries;
- acceptance basis where defined.

## Responsibilities

Separate responsibilities clearly.

### MORFRAC

State only responsibilities actually supported by the proposed scope.

### Client

State required client inputs, decisions, access, approvals or dependencies.

Do not silently assign obligations to either party.

## Schedule and Milestones

Where supported, state:

- start trigger;
- duration or dates;
- milestones;
- client dependencies;
- long-lead dependencies;
- review/approval dependencies;
- validity of schedule assumptions.

An internal planning date must not become a contractual delivery commitment without the applicable authority.

## Price and Options

State only commercially authorised values.

Include as applicable:

- currency;
- tax/VAT basis;
- unit or total price;
- option structure;
- quantities;
- inclusions;
- exclusions;
- validity;
- payment basis where approved.

Do not expose internal cost, margin or discount logic.

## Assumptions and Dependencies

Identify assumptions that materially affect:

- scope;
- price;
- schedule;
- technical feasibility;
- responsibilities;
- delivery.

## Exclusions

State material exclusions explicitly.

Do not use exclusions to contradict the main scope.

## Change Control

State how work outside the agreed scope will be reviewed and commercially addressed.

Do not imply that a change is automatically accepted or priced.

## Commercial and Legal Terms

Use only:

- approved MORFRAC terms;
- current reviewed project-specific terms;
- visible placeholders/status for unresolved legal review.

Do not invent missing terms.

## Validity

State offer validity only when supported by the current commercial decision.

## Related Links / Appendices

Include only appropriate client-safe supporting references.

Internal evidence links belong in the internal review pack.

# 4. Claims and Commitments

Material proposal statements include:

- price;
- scope;
- performance;
- specification;
- compliance;
- certification;
- schedule;
- availability;
- delivery;
- warranty;
- liability;
- confidentiality;
- intellectual property;
- acceptance;
- regulatory claims.

For each material statement, the internal review should be able to identify:

- exact proposed wording;
- source/revision;
- accountable owner/reviewer;
- applicable decision or approval;
- proposal location;
- relevant condition or limitation.

Do not strengthen source wording.

Examples:

| Supported source wording | Do not convert to |
| --- | --- |
| designed to | guaranteed to |
| target | will achieve |
| expected | guaranteed |
| estimated | fixed |
| subject to verification | verified |
| compatible subject to review | compatible |
| calculated | certified |
| tested sample | product-wide guarantee |

Preserve uncertainty and conditions.

---

# 5. Technical Claims

Technical statements must reflect current approved or reviewed technical evidence.

Do not invent or approve:

- dimensions;
- loads;
- factors of safety;
- materials;
- tolerances;
- performance;
- service life;
- compatibility;
- certification;
- standards compliance.

When technical review remains open, state the limitation visibly.

Examples:

- `SUBJECT TO ENGINEERING REVIEW`
- `PRELIMINARY`
- `TO BE VERIFIED`
- `BASED ON CURRENT SUPPLIED INPUTS`

Do not convert preliminary engineering into an unconditional client promise.

---

# 6. Commercial Claims

Commercial statements must distinguish:

- internal cost;
- price scenario;
- authorised selling price;
- discount;
- tax basis;
- payment basis;
- commercial commitment.

Only client-safe authorised values belong in the client draft.

Never expose:

- internal cost rates;
- margin;
- markup;
- confidential supplier cost;
- discount ceiling;
- commercial strategy;

unless explicitly intended for the authorised internal audience.

---

# 7. Schedule and Delivery

A proposal schedule should identify its basis.

Relevant inputs may include:

- engineering duration;
- client information/approval timing;
- procurement lead times;
- supplier quotations;
- manufacturing availability;
- testing;
- logistics;
- dependencies.

Distinguish:

- estimate;
- planning target;
- dependency;
- milestone;
- committed date.

Do not convert a planning estimate into a guaranteed delivery date.

Where critical information is missing, state the dependency rather than inventing a date.

# 8. Commercial and Legal Terms Checklist

Review and route as applicable:

- offer validity;
- currency;
- VAT/tax;
- price adjustment;
- payment schedule;
- deposits;
- late payment;
- delivery basis;
- freight;
- duty;
- customs;
- client dependencies;
- delay effects;
- scope changes;
- cancellation;
- acceptance;
- rejection;
- remedy;
- warranty scope;
- warranty start;
- warranty duration;
- warranty exclusions;
- liability;
- indemnity;
- insurance;
- consequential loss;
- confidentiality;
- publicity;
- background IP;
- project IP;
- licensing;
- data protection;
- security;
- regulatory/export controls;
- force majeure;
- termination;
- suspension;
- governing law;
- jurisdiction;
- document precedence.

Absence of a required term is a review item.

Do not invent contractual wording merely to make the proposal complete.

---

# 9. Assumptions, Dependencies and Exclusions

For each material assumption consider:

- source;
- owner;
- impact if false;
- affected scope;
- affected price;
- affected schedule;
- required resolution.

Typical categories include:

- client-supplied information;
- geometry/configuration;
- quantities;
- site access;
- interfaces;
- supplier availability;
- delivery basis;
- testing assumptions;
- regulatory assumptions;
- currency/tax basis.

Exclusions should be explicit and commercially understandable.

Do not hide known work inside vague assumptions.

---

# 10. Confidentiality and Audience

Classify outputs appropriately.

## CEO_CONFIDENTIAL_INTERNAL

May include:

- pricing strategy;
- margin;
- discount authority;
- supplier commercial terms;
- negotiation strategy;
- internal deviations;
- sensitive decision records.

## INTERNAL_PROJECT

May include project-operational information needed by authorised internal specialists without unnecessary confidential commercial detail.

## CLIENT_DRAFT_NOT_RELEASED

Client-safe proposed content that has not been externally released.

## HUMAN_RELEASE_READY

A verified client-safe package that has completed the required review/release process and is ready for an authorised human to transmit.

`HUMAN_RELEASE_READY` does not mean sent.

Before a handoff consider:

- recipient;
- purpose;
- minimum necessary information;
- classification;
- authority.

Do not infer access authority from an agent or employee name.

---

# 11. Proposal ID and Version

Use a stable proposal ID such as:

`PROP-YYYY-NNN`

Use monotonic versions:

`v01`
`v02`
`v03`

Do not overwrite a prior version.

A revision should identify:

- prior/baseline version;
- reason for change;
- requester;
- changed sources;
- affected sections;
- changed commercial values;
- changed technical scope;
- required re-reviews;
- current status.

---

# 12. Canonical Proposal Paths

Use only the existing project proposal area.

## Client Draft

`08_PROJECTS/Active/<Project_Name>/06_Proposals/Client_Drafts/<Proposal_ID>_<Version>_DRAFT.md`

## Internal Review Pack

`08_PROJECTS/Active/<Project_Name>/06_Proposals/Internal_Review/<Proposal_ID>_<Version>_INTERNAL.md`

Use the same proposal ID/version for the pair.

Do not add ad-hoc client names or alternate filename structures.

Project Manager owns the project/proposal folder structure.

Proposal must not create or repair project folders.

---

# 13. Version Immutability

Once a proposal version has been persistently saved:

- do not overwrite it;
- do not edit it in place;
- do not silently change metadata;
- do not silently increment the version after a collision.

If content changes, prepare a new version.

A changed:

- body;
- metadata;
- source set;
- filename;
- proposal ID;
- version;
- path;
- audience;

requires a new current save plan under the applicable workflow.

A collision is a blocker, not permission for automatic version increment.

---

# 14. Review Status

Proposal readiness should distinguish at least:

- technical review;
- schedule review;
- price/commercial review;
- legal review;
- client-data completeness;
- confidentiality review.

An unresolved required review prevents release readiness but does not necessarily prevent continued drafting.

Use scoped blocking.

---

# 15. Internal Review Pack

The internal pack should make the client draft auditable.

Where relevant record:

| Area | Review content |
| --- | --- |
| Technical | source/revision, reviewer, open assumptions |
| Scope | included/excluded work, responsibilities |
| Schedule | basis, dependencies, reviewer |
| Price | authorised price reference, currency/tax/validity |
| Commercial | options, payment/validity decisions |
| Legal | terms source, deviations, unresolved review |
| Claims | claim wording and supporting source |
| Confidentiality | information intentionally omitted from client version |
| Changes | differences from prior proposal version |
| Release | outstanding conditions |

Do not insert confidential internal review information into the client draft.

---

# 16. QA Before Save or Release

Check:

## Identity

- correct project;
- proposal ID;
- version;
- client/project identity.

## Scope

- scope consistent with source;
- deliverables explicit;
- exclusions visible;
- responsibilities clear.

## Technical

- no unsupported claims;
- correct revision/configuration;
- limitations preserved.

## Schedule

- dates/durations supported;
- dependencies visible;
- no accidental guarantee.

## Commercial

- price authorised for client use;
- currency correct;
- tax basis stated;
- options internally consistent;
- validity supported.

## Legal

- terms source identified;
- deviations visible;
- unresolved review not hidden.

## Confidentiality

- no internal cost/margin/supplier-sensitive data in client draft;
- client-safe links only.

## Revision

- correct monotonic version;
- no overwrite;
- changes from prior version understood.

---

# 17. Release Boundary

Persistent save and external release are separate states.

A saved draft remains:

`NOT RELEASED`

Only the authorised human release process may:

- transmit;
- email;
- upload;
- publish;
- sign;
- submit;
- negotiate;
- accept.

Do not remove `DRAFT` merely because a file was saved.

Do not claim that a proposal was sent unless an authoritative external-action record confirms it.

---

# 18. Quality Standard

A high-quality proposal allows a reviewer and client to understand:

- what problem is being addressed;
- what MORFRAC proposes;
- what is included;
- what is excluded;
- who is responsible for what;
- what assumptions matter;
- what the expected schedule basis is;
- what the authorised commercial basis is;
- what conditions remain;
- which claims are supported;
- which review or decision remains open.

Conciseness is preferred over boilerplate.

Do not add sections that do not materially help the specific proposal.
