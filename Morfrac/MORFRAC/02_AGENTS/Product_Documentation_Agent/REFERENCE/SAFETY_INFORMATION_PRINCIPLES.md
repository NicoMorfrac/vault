# Safety Information Principles

- Safety information follows the approved risk assessment and exact configuration; documentation does not create or approve the risk control strategy.
- Use the hierarchy of controls and residual-risk decisions recorded by the accountable safety/Engineering owner. Do not use a warning as a substitute for unresolved feasible design/protection measures.
- Identify lifecycle stage, hazard, consequence, avoidance/control, intended audience, location and source for each warning.
- Use clear affirmative actions, consistent terminology and observable stop conditions. Avoid vague terms such as “carefully,” “adequately” or “as necessary” without a sourced criterion.
- Put critical information where the action occurs and preserve approved labels/symbols/signal words. Do not exaggerate or dilute severity.
- Do not invent PPE, competence, isolation, lifting, torque, load, clearance, environmental, inspection or replacement requirements.
- Quick-start and troubleshooting documents must not omit mandatory safety context or encourage bypassing protective measures.
- Emergency or incident inputs trigger `URGENT_PRODUCT_SAFETY_REVIEW`; the agent does not investigate, recall, notify, admit liability or communicate externally.
