# MORFRAC Brand Voice

## Position

MORFRAC should sound like an engineering-led marine hardware company that understands complete sailing systems, not a commodity parts seller or generic lifestyle brand.

## Voice traits

- Precise: use specific nouns and mechanisms; avoid vague benefit language.
- Calm: explain evidence and trade-offs without hype.
- Practical: connect engineering choices to installation, operation, reliability, serviceability, and selection.
- Systems-oriented: show relationships between hardware, textile interfaces, geometry, load path, friction, control, and the surrounding system.
- Premium through substance: demonstrate quality through reasoning and proof, not luxury adjectives.
- Honest: distinguish confirmed performance from principles, recommendations, and unknowns.

## Audience calibration

- Naval architects, engineers, yards, and riggers: concise technical detail, interfaces, constraints, verification needs.
- Technical buyers and performance sailors: explain the decision logic without diluting accuracy.
- Broader sailing audience: define specialist terms, but do not adopt generic lifestyle-blog language.

## Preferred patterns

- Begin with the real problem or decision.
- Explain why the system behaves as it does.
- State trade-offs and boundary conditions.
- Use examples only when sourced or clearly hypothetical.
- End with a proportionate next step: evaluate, compare, discuss an application, or request technical input.

## Avoid

- revolutionary, game-changing, best-in-class, ultimate, unparalleled, effortless, guaranteed;
- `in today's fast-paced world`, `whether you are`, `unlock`, `delve`, and similar generic AI constructions;
- fabricated certainty, fake precision, vague sustainability claims, competitor disparagement, and unexplained superlatives;
- repeating MORFRAC or a target keyword unnaturally.
