# Fracture and Fatigue

Preserve fracture faces from contact, marking, cleaning and corrosion. Document origin candidates, crack direction, macroscopic deformation, section loss, beach/arrest features, secondary cracks and mating surfaces without overclaiming.

Distinguish ductile/brittle appearance, overload, fatigue, environmentally assisted cracking and secondary breakage as hypotheses until qualified fractography/material evidence supports them.

Fatigue assessment requires stress range/history, cycles, geometry/notch, surface, residual stress, environment and material data. A visually smooth region or line pattern alone is not proof.
