# MORAAAAA-91 Summary Report

Date: 2026-05-24
Issue: Rigger and Refit Yard Workflow Bottlenecks

---

# Executive Summary

This investigation found two recurring workflow bottlenecks with clear mechanical-retrofit relevance:

1. Mast-handling access and staging constraints repeatedly determine whether rigging, furling, and inspection jobs are practical, safe, and economically bundleable.
2. Rig-specific measurement and sequencing uncertainty repeatedly creates fabrication rework, adjustment-margin problems, and ownership disputes during standing-rigging and furler-related refits.

The strongest convergence is not around a missing piece of hardware. It is around uncertainty transfer. Access uncertainty and measurement uncertainty both shift risk onto riggers and refit yards before they can even execute the core mechanical work.

---

# Strategic Interpretation

## Evidence-backed interpretation

- The recurring pain is operational, not cosmetic.
- The highest-friction jobs are geometry-sensitive and sequence-sensitive.
- Much of the value appears to sit upstream of fabrication in planning, validation, and scope control.

## What this supports

- Pre-job mast-access planning
- Retrofit intake standardization
- Geometry and measurement validation
- Liability-conscious installation-support documentation
- Bundled work sequencing for mast-down service windows

## What this does not prove

- Market size
- willingness to pay
- whether the best commercial form is a service, package, or partnership

---

# Pattern Convergence

## Recurring systemic causes

- Legacy boats lack dependable as-built rigging documentation.
- Access infrastructure varies widely between yards.
- Mast lifts are expensive enough that installers batch work, which increases sequencing pressure.
- Furlers and standing-rigging changes reduce tolerance for measurement errors because adjustment room is finite.

## Why the convergence matters

- These are recurring B2B workflow problems that sit close to MORFRAC's retrofit-engineering and installation-support scope.
- The opportunity appears more bounded and repeatable when framed as engineering-risk reduction than when framed as open-ended custom rigging consultancy.

# Final Assessment

Evidence suggests recurring value in bounded support layers around rigging refits: access planning, geometry capture, sequence control, and installation-readiness validation.

Commercial validation remains limited. What is well supported is repeated technical friction that consumes yard capacity, creates callback risk, and increases liability ambiguity around high-consequence mechanical retrofit work.
