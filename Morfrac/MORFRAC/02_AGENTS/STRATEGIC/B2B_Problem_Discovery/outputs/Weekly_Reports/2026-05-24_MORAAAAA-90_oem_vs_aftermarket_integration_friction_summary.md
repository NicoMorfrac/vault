Date: 2026-05-24
Issue: OEM vs Aftermarket Integration Friction in Performance Sailing Systems

---

# Executive Summary

This investigation found five recurring OEM-versus-aftermarket friction patterns in sailing-system modernization:

1. Proprietary network and connector fragmentation turns device additions into backbone redesign work.
2. Mixed-brand autopilot integrations often pass data without delivering full command or setup functionality.
3. OEM steering geometry frequently forces custom mechanical engineering during autopilot retrofits.
4. Incomplete wiring and power-topology documentation makes reverse engineering a prerequisite to upgrades.
5. Product obsolescence and support boundaries push owners toward wider same-brand replacement rather than controlled adaptation.

The convergence is strongest where steering control, network topology, and undocumented installed conditions intersect. That reinforces MORFRAC's broader strategic theme that bounded engineering-risk reduction is valuable in marine retrofit environments.

# Strategic Interpretation

## Evidence-backed interpretation

- The strongest recurring pain is not generic dissatisfaction with marine electronics.
- The strongest recurring pain is integration uncertainty at the boundary between old installed systems and new desired functionality.
- Technical risk concentrates around three interfaces:
  - network/protocol interface
  - steering/mechanical interface
  - documentation/support interface

## What this supports

- Vendor-neutral integration engineering
- Retrofit scoping and validation
- Steering-drive adaptation support
- Electrical/network documentation normalization
- Supported migration planning for aging premium sailing systems

## What this does not prove

- Market size
- willingness to pay
- productizable software demand
- preference for any specific commercial packaging

---

# Pattern Convergence

## Recurring systemic causes

- OEM installations are optimized for original delivery, not later neutrality.
- Partial standards create false expectations of full compatibility.
- Legacy boats and legacy electronics lose documentation and support before they lose physical utility.
- Retrofit jobs blend mechanics, controls, wiring, and commissioning in ways that commodity installers may underscope.

## Why the convergence matters

- These are not isolated owner annoyances.
- The observed friction repeatedly lands on technically demanding, high-consequence systems: autopilots, steering linkages, power topology, and instrument networks.
- That makes the opportunity space more aligned with engineering services, validation, technical partnerships, and installation-support systems than with simple product retail.
