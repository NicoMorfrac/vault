A dogbone joint looks simple. It rarely is.

In performance sailing, the real question is not whether a joint can connect two components. It is whether it can do so while preserving load path integrity, articulation, alignment, and fatigue life under highly variable conditions.

That is where dogbone geometry becomes an engineering problem, not just a hardware detail.

A well-designed dogbone joint must manage axial load, angular misalignment, and cyclic movement without creating unintended stress concentrations. Material selection, surface finish, tolerance control, and pin interface all matter. In racing applications, small compromises here can show up later as play in the system, reduced efficiency, or premature wear in surrounding components.

The challenge is amplified when the joint sits inside a lightweight structure where every gram and every millimeter has consequences. Overbuild it, and you pay in weight and friction. Under-design it, and you pay in reliability.

That balance is central to modern sailing hardware design: minimum mass, maximum load certainty, and predictable behavior under dynamic conditions.

At MORFRAC, this is the kind of problem we focus on across structural sailing systems, textile interfaces, and custom hardware. The detail is never just the detail. It is the difference between acceptable and optimized.
