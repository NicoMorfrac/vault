A dogbone looks simple. In a performance rig, it is anything but.

When a sailing dogbone is doing its job properly, it is managing load transfer, alignment, and articulation in a part of the system where millimeters matter. The difference between a clean, efficient connection and a compromised one is often found in the geometry: bearing surface, pin interface, textile abrasion, and how the load enters and exits under dynamic conditions.

For performance sailing, the key question is not just strength. It is how the component behaves under repeated cycling, shock loading, and off-axis movement. A good dogbone should reduce unnecessary hardware stack-up, maintain reliable articulation, and avoid introducing friction or point loading that can accelerate wear elsewhere in the system.

This is why engineering choices around material selection, radius control, and interface design matter. In modern sail systems, small inefficiencies compound quickly. A few grams saved are useful, but a few percent gained in load path efficiency, durability, or consistency is often more valuable.

At MORFRAC, this is the level we work at: designing hardware and structural sailing systems with the load case first, then the hardware geometry, then the weight.
