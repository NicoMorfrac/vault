A padeye is rarely the failure point people see first. It is usually the point where load, geometry, laminate, and hardware design all meet.

In performance sailing, that matters. A padeye is not just an attachment point. It is a load transfer element. If the axis is wrong, if the footprint is too small, if the bearing surface does not match the real direction of load, the structure pays for it later in fatigue, distortion, or local damage.

The engineering challenge is simple to describe and difficult to execute: keep weight low while managing high, often cyclic, multi-directional loads. On modern boats, that means thinking beyond static strength. It means understanding load paths, laminate reinforcement, backing geometry, corrosion interfaces, and how the hardware behaves under dynamic trim and shock loading.

This is where custom hardware earns its place. A padeye designed for a specific system can reduce secondary structure, improve alignment, and deliver a cleaner transfer of load into the boat. That is not just a structural benefit. It is a performance gain.

At MORFRAC, we approach padeyes the same way we approach every structural sailing component: as an engineered interface, not a standard part.
