A dogbone is a small component with a disproportionately large job.

In performance sailing, it is often the simplest-looking parts that carry the most critical loads, define the articulation geometry, and determine how cleanly a system transfers force under dynamic conditions. A well-designed dogbone is not just a connector. It is part of the structural logic of the entire assembly.

That means the real design questions are rarely about shape alone. They are about load paths, bearing alignment, fatigue behavior, material choice, and how the part behaves when the system is not perfectly loaded. Offshore cycles, shock loads, and constant movement expose weaknesses quickly. If the geometry is wrong, the hardware may still function, but it will not optimize efficiency or durability.

In high-performance sailing, that difference matters. Every gram, every millimeter of articulation, and every reduction in unwanted friction or slop contributes to a cleaner, more reliable system. The best solutions are usually the ones that disappear into the structure and let the load do its work without drama.
