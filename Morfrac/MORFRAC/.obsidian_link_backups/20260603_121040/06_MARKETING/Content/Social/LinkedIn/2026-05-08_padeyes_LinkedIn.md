A padeye looks simple until it becomes the highest-loaded point in the system.

In performance sailing, padeyes are not just attachment points. They are load-transfer components. Their geometry, material choice, laminate interface, fastener pattern, and orientation all influence how loads enter the structure and how much margin remains when the boat is driven hard.

The engineering challenge is rarely the static load number. It is the combination of cyclic loading, off-axis load cases, local crushing, peel risk, and the way the surrounding structure behaves under real sailing conditions. A well-designed padeye should distribute load cleanly, minimize stress concentrations, and maintain stiffness without adding unnecessary mass.

This is where detail matters. Oversizing can be as problematic as under-designing. Poor alignment can create secondary bending. The wrong interface between hardware and structure can reduce the effective strength of an otherwise robust component.

At MORFRAC, we approach padeyes as part of a system, not a standalone fitting. That means thinking about load paths, installation constraints, material behavior, and how the hardware performs over time in a demanding marine environment.

For performance projects, the real question is not whether a padeye is strong enough in isolation. It is whether the entire load path is optimized.

How do you approach padeye design: minimum mass, maximum stiffness, or serviceability over the full life of the boat?
